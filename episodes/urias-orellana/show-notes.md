---

## Bottom line

The Court held unanimously that the INA requires **substantial-evidence review** when a court of appeals evaluates the BIA’s determination that undisputed facts do not amount to *persecution* under § 1101(a)(42)(A). That deferential standard applies to the **entire** persecution inquiry — both the agency’s factual findings and its application of the statutory standard — not just discrete “findings of fact” reviewed de novo as a mixed question. For asylum practitioners, the practical effect is that reversing a BIA persecution determination on petition for review remains difficult: the court asks whether any reasonable adjudicator would be *compelled* to reach a contrary conclusion.

## Episode summary

In this dry-run episode of *Inside Black Boxes*, we walk through *Urias-Orellana v. Bondi* — a unanimous March 2026 decision resolving a circuit split on the standard of review for BIA persecution determinations. Salvadoran petitioners presented credible testimony about targeted violence, but the IJ and BIA denied asylum. The Court did not revisit the persecution standard itself; it held that § 1252(b)(4)(B) requires substantial-evidence review of the agency’s conclusion that the facts did not establish persecution. We cover the holding, Elias-Zacarias, and what this means for asylum appeals going forward.

---

## Key holdings

1. **Substantial-evidence standard applies** — § 1252(b)(4)(B) requires courts of appeals to review the agency’s persecution determination — facts plus application of § 1101(a)(42)(A) — for substantial evidence. (Slip op. at 7, 13.)
2. **Elias-Zacarias codified** — Congress’s IIRIRA amendments, including § 1252(b)(4)(B), codified the deferential standard from *INS v. Elias-Zacarias*, 502 U. S. 478 (1992), rather than expanding judicial review. (Slip op. at 8–11.)
3. **Mixed question ≠ de novo** — The persecution determination is a mixed question, but Congress intended the **whole** determination to receive deference under § 1252(b)(4)(B). (Slip op. at 11–12.)
4. **Wilkinson / Guerrero-Lasprilla distinguished** — Those cases address jurisdictional review under § 1252(a), not the standard of review under § 1252(b)(4). (Slip op. at 12–13.)

---

## Practitioner takeaways

- [ ] **Frame appeals around compulsion** — Petitioners must show the record compels a contrary persecution finding; substantial evidence governs the BIA’s application of law to facts.
- [ ] **Do not rely on de novo “mixed question” theories** for persecution determinations after this decision.
- [ ] **Pure legal challenges preserved** — The Court noted it did not decide the appropriate persecution standard because petitioners did not challenge the agency’s legal threshold; purely legal questions about that standard may still receive de novo review (SG concession). (Slip op. at 5 n.1.)
- [ ] **Credibility ≠ persecution** — An IJ may credit testimony and still deny asylum if the harm does not meet the persecution threshold — with deference on review.
- [ ] **Update circuit-split boilerplate** — Treat persecution review as uniformly substantial-evidence post-*Urias-Orellana*.

## What the Court did not decide

- The **substantive persecution standard** applied by the agency (First Circuit’s “so menacing as to cause significant actual suffering or harm” threshold for death threats) — petitioners did not challenge it. (Slip op. at 5 n.1.)
- Petitioners’ **Loper Bright** argument for de novo review — Court found it unnecessary given § 1252(b)(4)(B)’s deferential mandate. (Slip op. at 12 n.6.)
- Whether petitioners satisfied persecution on the merits — only the standard of review was before the Court.

## Key quotes

> “We hold that § 1252(b)(4)(B) requires courts to review the entirety of the agency’s conclusions—both the underlying factual findings and the application of the INA to those findings—for substantial evidence.”  
> — Jackson, J., slip op. at 7

> “The INA requires application of the substantial-evidence standard to the agency’s determination whether a given set of undisputed facts rises to the level of persecution under § 1101(a)(42)(A).”  
> — Syllabus

> “[T]o obtain judicial reversal of the agency’s persecution determination, an asylum applicant must show that the evidence he presented was so compelling that no reasonable factfinder could fail to find the requisite fear of persecution.”  
> — Jackson, J., quoting *Elias-Zacarias*, slip op. at 8
