# Machine Discretion — Administrative Law Frame

> Provisional. Not a treatise. Captures how the project sits inside administrative law.  
> Last updated: 2026-07-26

---

## Why this document exists

The author has stated that the problem of machines surfacing facts outside an officer’s ordinary view is **an administrative law question** — not only a technology ethics slogan, and not only professional-responsibility talk in the abstract.

Immigration benefits adjudication (USCIS and related records) is the working theater.

---

## Working claim from Interview Q04

When a machine surfaces a fact outside the view an officer would ordinarily have:

- It can be **better adjudication** (more complete truth under the same legal standards) — **A**
- **And** it can be **something else depending on who you represent** — **A + C**

So the evaluation is **role-relative** inside an administrative proceeding:

| Seat (illustrative) | Possible stance (not yet author-locked) |
|---------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Applicant / petitioner (or counsel) | Extra-silo fact may help or hurt; “better” is not neutral |
| Agency / adjudicating officer | Completeness vs. record limits, burden, efficiency, consistency |
| Reviewing body / court | Whether the decision rests on a lawful process and adequate grounds |
| Public / systemic critic | Legitimacy of human fiction + machine competence |

What “C” is on each seat is **not yet specified** by the author — only that it depends on representation.
