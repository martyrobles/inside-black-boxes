Substantial Evidence and Asylum: Urias-Orellana v. Bondi

Unanimous Court holds that § 1252(b)(4)(B) requires substantial-evidence review of the BIA's persecution determination — facts and application of law together.

Bottom line

The Court held unanimously that the INA requires substantial-evidence review when a court of appeals evaluates the BIA's determination that undisputed facts do not amount to persecution under § 1101(a)(42)(A). That deferential standard applies to the entire persecution inquiry — both the agency's factual findings and its application of the statutory standard.

For asylum practitioners, the practical effect is unchanged in direction but clarified in doctrine: reversing a BIA persecution determination on petition for review remains difficult. The court asks whether any reasonable adjudicator would be compelled to reach a contrary conclusion.

Key holdings

  • Substantial-evidence standard applies — § 1252(b)(4)(B) requires review of the agency's persecution determination for substantial evidence, including application of § 1101(a)(42)(A) to the facts.
  • Elias-Zacarias codified — IIRIRA's § 1252(b)(4)(B) codified the deferential standard from INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U. S. 478 (1992).
  • Mixed question ≠ de novo — Congress intended the whole persecution determination to receive deference, not just discrete findings of fact.
  • Wilkinson / Guerrero-Lasprilla distinguished — those cases address § 1252(a) jurisdictional review, not the § 1252(b)(4) standard of review.

Practitioner checklist

  • Frame appeals around compulsion — not clear error or de novo mixed-question review.
  • Distinguish pure legal challenges to the persecution standard from application of an agreed standard to facts.
  • Update circuit-split boilerplate; treat persecution review as uniformly substantial-evidence.
  • Counsel clients that credible testimony alone may not establish persecution under the applicable framework.

What the Court did not decide

  • The substantive persecution standard (First Circuit's "so menacing" threshold for death threats).
  • Petitioners' Loper Bright argument for de novo review.
  • Whether petitioners satisfied persecution on the merits.
