# Machine Discretion — Philosophy

> Provisional. Claims here are working theses until locked in `STATUS.md`.  
> Last updated: 2026-07-25

---

## Core tension (working)

Under the current legal framework, **only humans** can exercise **independent professional judgment and discretion**. Adjudicating government officials also exercise judgment in connection with benefits.

In practice, that work is increasingly not done by humans. It is done by **generative AI**.

**Machine Discretion** names the collision between:

1. A legal and ethical order that assigns judgment and discretion to human professionals (and human adjudicators), and
2. An operational reality in which generative systems perform more of the work that looks like — and functions as — that judgment.

The open philosophical and practical question is how this implicates **ethical standards** and **legal standards**.

## Working distinctions (not yet settled)

| Term | Working sense | Still open |
|------|---------------|------------|
| Independent professional judgment | Legally and ethically assigned to humans | What “independence” requires when tools are generative |
| Discretion | Judgment under uncertainty, with accountability | Whether a machine can have it, simulate it, or only displace it |
| Adjudicative judgment | Official decisions tied to benefits / rights | How far genAI already substitutes for the official |
| Generative AI | Systems producing the analytical or decisional work product | Tool vs. decision-maker vs. laundered authority |
