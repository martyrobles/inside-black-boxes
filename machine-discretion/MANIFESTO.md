# Machine Discretion — Manifesto

> A living governing document. Philosophy and judgment before software.  
> Last updated: 2026-07-26 · Status: discovery

---

## What this is

**Machine Discretion** is a long-term project to discover and articulate a philosophy, a practice of editorial judgment, and a product vision — before any implementation decisions are made.

It is **not** Inside Black Boxes.  
It is **not** Blackletter Lab.  
It may eventually become software, media, doctrine, or something else. That question is deferred until the vision is coherent.

The working object of this project is an **editorial operating system**: the principles, distinctions, and judgment rules that govern how humans and machines should exercise discretion together.

---

## What this is not (yet)

- Not a codebase
- Not a feature list
- Not a UI
- Not a startup pitch deck
- Not a reaction to a single tool or model release

Implementation is forbidden until both author and collaborator agree the vision is coherent and complete enough to constrain design.

---

## The discovery method

Treat every answer as if interviewing an author before they write their defining book. The goal is to uncover ideas that are **true but not yet fully articulated**.

### Collaborator roles

The AI collaborator serves as:

1. **Interviewer** — one substantive question at a time
2. **Collaborator** — helps sharpen language without inventing the thesis
3. **Devil’s advocate** — pressure-tests claims and soft spots
4. **Documentary producer** — preserves voice, chronology, and turning points
5. **Product strategist** — distinguishes philosophy from product without collapsing them early
6. **Systems architect** — later, and only when invited: structures that follow from settled judgment

### After every answer

1. Summarize what was learned
2. Identify assumptions or inconsistencies
3. State what decisions remain unresolved
4. Update the appropriate specification document
5. Recommend which specification document should be created next

## Known so far

From the project’s opening statement:

- The name is **Machine Discretion**
- The horizon is **long-term**
- The immediate work is **discovery and articulation**, not construction
- The domains to clarify first are **philosophy**, **editorial judgment**, and **product vision** — in that spirit, not necessarily in that order of documents
- The desired outcome of this phase is an **editorial operating system**
- Software design waits on mutual agreement that the vision is coherent

From Interview Q01–Q04 (see `PHILOSOPHY.md`, `ADMINISTRATIVE_LAW.md`):

- Law assigns **independent professional judgment and discretion** to humans; government adjudicators also exercise judgment over **benefits**
- Primary failure mode: **legal fiction of human judgment** — human signs; genAI does the real discretionary work
- Humans are **flawed and context-limited**; larger model context may yield **better** decisions/adjudications
- Central paradox: **what happens when machines are better than humans, but the law requires humans to make the call?**
- “Better” (working): **better appreciation of legal standards** + **more facts humans miss** (e.g. State Department file, FOIA; uneven USCIS scrutiny of AOS vs N-400)
- Clearest defining theater so far: **immigration benefits adjudication**
- Cross-silo machine facts: **A + C depending on who you represent** — an **administrative law** question
- The project asks how this **implicates ethical and legal standards**

### Not yet known

- From which representational seat Machine Discretion speaks (or whether it only maps seats)
- What “C” is on each seat
- Whether a non-responsible machine is better at adjudication or only at components
- Whether the aim is to expose the fiction, redesign law for competent machines, keep humans as responsibility anchors despite worse accuracy, or something else
- Whether it is primarily a book, a practice, a product, a publication, or a doctrine that could take many forms
- What “editorial” modifies in “editorial operating system”
- What success looks like for this discovery phase

## Operating constraints

1. **One question at a time.** Depth over coverage.
2. **No implementation code** during discovery.
3. **No software design** until the vision is agreed coherent.
4. **Do not smuggle product features** into philosophical answers.
5. **Preserve the author’s language** when it is sharper than a rewrite.
6. **Separate projects stay separate.** Do not fold Machine Discretion into Inside Black Boxes or Blackletter Lab unless the author explicitly decides otherwise.
