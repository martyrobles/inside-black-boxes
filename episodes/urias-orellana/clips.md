**Title (internal):** `urias-orellana-clip-01-holding`  
**Target length:** 30–45 seconds

### On-screen quote (verbatim)

> The INA requires application of the substantial-evidence standard to the agency’s determination whether a given set of undisputed facts rises to the level of persecution under § 1101(a)(42)(A).

**Source:** Syllabus, *Urias-Orellana v. Bondi*, No. 24-777 (U.S. Mar. 4, 2026).

### Caption text (social post)

Unanimous SCOTUS: courts of appeals must apply substantial-evidence review when the BIA decides whether facts amount to “persecution” under the INA — not a more searching de novo standard.

## Clip 2 — What “substantial evidence” covers

**Title (internal):** `urias-orellana-clip-02-scope`  
**Target length:** 45–60 seconds  
**Platform:** LinkedIn

### On-screen quote (verbatim)

> We hold that § 1252(b)(4)(B) requires courts to review the entirety of the agency’s conclusions—both the underlying factual findings and the application of the INA to those findings—for substantial evidence.

**Source:** Slip op. at 7, *Urias-Orellana v. Bondi* (Jackson, J.).

### Caption text (social post)

The Court rejected the idea that courts can apply de novo review when the BIA applies the persecution standard to undisputed facts. Facts **and** application of law get substantial-evidence review.

#ImmigrationLaw #Asylum #SCOTUS

### Voiceover hook (host)

“Circuit courts were split on this for years. Jackson’s answer: the whole persecution determination — not just isolated facts — gets § 1252(b)(4)(B) deference.”

## Clip 3 — Monday morning for asylum appeals

**Title (internal):** `urias-orellana-clip-03-practice`  
**Target length:** 45–60 seconds  
**Platform:** LinkedIn

### On-screen quote (verbatim)

> [T]o obtain judicial reversal of the agency’s persecution determination, an asylum applicant must show that the evidence he presented was so compelling that no reasonable factfinder could fail to find the requisite fear of persecution.

**Source:** Slip op. at 8 (quoting *INS v. Elias-Zacarias*, 502 U. S. 478, 483–484 (1992)).

### Caption text (social post)

*Urias-Orellana* did not lower the bar for asylum seekers on the merits — it clarified how hard reversal is on petition for review. Frame appeals around **compulsion**, not clear error.

*Inside Black Boxes* — SCOTUS immigration coverage for practitioners.

### Voiceover hook (host)

“If you’re briefing asylum in the courts of appeals, Elias-Zacarias is still the game. This decision says Congress never took the referees off the field.”
